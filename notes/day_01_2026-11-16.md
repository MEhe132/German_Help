## Part 1: The 4 Special Characters (Umlauts & Eszett)

In addition to the standard 26 English letters, German has **4 special characters**:

| Character | Name | Sound Trick / Approximate Sound | German Example | Meaning |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ä** | A-Umlaut | Sounds like "e" in **"bed"** or "ai" in **"air"** | *Äpfel* (EP-fel)<br>*Mädchen* (MED-chen) | Apples<br>Girl |
| **ö** | O-Umlaut | Shape your lips into an "O", but try to say **"ay"** (like the *u* in "burn") | *schön* (shurn)<br>*hören* (HER-ren) | Beautiful / Nice<br>To hear |
| **ü** | U-Umlaut | Pout your lips forward like whistling, and say **"ee"** | *über* (UE-ber)<br>*Tschüss* (tchooss) | Over / Above<br>Bye |
| **ß** | Eszett (sharp s) | Pronounced exactly like a sharp **double "ss"** | *Straße* (SHTRAH-seh)<br>*heiß* (hice) | Street<br>Hot |

> [!TIP]
> **What if your keyboard doesn't have ä, ö, ü, ß?**  
> In German, you can legally write:
> - `ä` as **ae** (e.g. *Mädchen* → *Maedchen*)
> - `ö` as **oe** (e.g. *schön* → *schoen*)
> - `ü` as **ue** (e.g. *über* → *ueber*)
> - `ß` as **ss** (e.g. *Straße* → *Strasse*)

## Part 2: The 5 Golden Pronunciation Rules

German pronunciation is 99% phonetic—words are spoken exactly as spelled, unlike English! Remember these 5 rules:

### Rule 1: The "ei" vs "ie" Trap (Most Common Beginner Mistake!)
Always pronounce the **second letter's English name**:
- **e + i** → say English **"I"** (Eye)  
  *Examples:* `nein` (pronounced "nine" = No), `mein` (pronounced "mine" = My), `Wein` ("vine" = Wine)
- **i + e** → say English **"E"** (Ee)  
  *Examples:* `sie` (pronounced "zee" = She/They), `hier` (pronounced "heer" = Here), `Bier` ("beer" = Beer)

### Rule 2: The "W" and "V" Switch
- **W** sounds like English **V**  
  *Wasser* (VAH-ser = Water), *Wo* (VOH = Where), *Wir* (VEER = We)
- **V** sounds like English **F**  
  *Vogel* (FOH-gel = Bird), *Vier* (FEER = Four), *Vater* (FAH-ter = Father)

### Rule 3: The "Z" is always "TS"
Every time you see a **Z**, make a crisp **"ts"** sound like the end of "ca**ts**":
- *Zug* (TSOOK = Train)
- *Zwei* (TSVYE = Two)
- *Zeit* (TSYTE = Time)

### Rule 4: The Soft "S" at the Start
An **S** at the beginning of a word before a vowel sounds like a soft **Z**:
- *Sonne* (ZON-neh = Sun)
- *Salat* (zah-LAHT = Salad)

### Rule 5: "SP" and "ST" become "SHP" and "SHT"
When `sp` or `st` appear at the start of a word/syllable, pronounce the `s` as **"sh"**:
- *Sport* (pronounced **SHPORT**)
- *Straße* (pronounced **SHTRAH-seh**)
- *Student* (pronounced **SHTOO-dent**)

## Part 3: Everyday Greetings (Die Begrüßungen)

### Time-Based Greetings
| German | Pronunciation Guide | English Meaning | Usage Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hallo!** | HAH-lo | Hello / Hi | Any time, informal & friendly |
| **Guten Morgen!** | GOO-ten MOR-gen | Good morning | From waking up until ~11:00 AM |
| **Guten Tag!** | GOO-ten TAHK | Good day / Hello | Main daytime greeting (~11 AM to 6 PM) |
| **Guten Abend!** | GOO-ten AH-bent | Good evening | After ~6:00 PM |
| **Gute Nacht!** | GOO-teh NAKHT | Good night | **Only** when going to bed or leaving late |

> [!NOTE]
> Why is it *Guten Morgen* with an **-n**, but *Gute Nacht* **without an -n**?  
> Because in German:
> - *der Morgen* is masculine (becomes *guten* in greeting)
> - *die Nacht* is feminine (stays *gute*)  
> *(We will explore this magical grammar secret in Phase 3!)*

### Saying Goodbye (Die Verabschiedungen)
| German | Pronunciation | English Meaning | Usage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tschüss!** | TCHOOSS | Bye! | Casual / Everyday with anyone |
| **Auf Wiedersehen!** | owf VEE-der-zay-en | Goodbye! (Until we see each other again) | Formal / Professional |
| **Bis bald!** | bis BALT | See you soon! | Informal |

## Part 4: Your First Sentence: Saying Your Name

To introduce yourself in German, use the magical verb **heißen** (to be called):

$$\text{Ich heiße } [\text{Your Name}]$$

- *Ich* = I (pronounced like "ikh" with a soft whispery breath)
- *heiße* = am called (pronounced "HY-seh")

**Example:**
> *"Hallo! Ich heiße Mehedi."*  
> (Hello! My name is Mehedi.)

To ask someone their name casually:
> *"Wie heißt du?"* (VEE HYST DOO? = What is your name?)
